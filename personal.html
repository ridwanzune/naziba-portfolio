the short version

I'm endlessly curious, quietly observant, deeply emotional, and happiest when I'm mid-conversation, mid-journey, or mid-way through figuring something out. I care more about the process than the finish line — always have.

the world between three places

I was born and raised in Bangladesh, spent three years building an independent life in the U.S. during my undergrad, and the last two navigating the beautiful chaos of London while finishing my master's. Somewhere between those three very different worlds, I stopped fearing the unfamiliar and started chasing it.

I've learned that "home" isn't really a place for me — it's a feeling I know how to build, wherever I land.

what makes me, me

I ask too many questions. Not the small-talk kind — the "wait, but why does it actually work that way" kind. If something catches my interest, I can't just know the fact; I need to understand the why underneath it. This has made me a lifelong learner in the least academic sense of the word — I just genuinely love figuring things out.

I notice things. A shift in someone's tone, a detail no one else caught, the thing left unsaid in a room full of things being said. I've always been like this — deeply attentive to people, and almost as attentive to my own inner world. I reflect a lot, probably more than is strictly necessary, but it's given me a level of self-awareness I wouldn't trade for anything.

✨

I love people, food, culture, and everything in between. Travel, for me, isn't about checking places off a list — it's about sitting at someone's table, hearing their stories, tasting how they experience the world.

endlessly fascinated by psychology, nature, and how many different ways there are to live a life.

where I find meaning

I care, intensely. Helping people, contributing something useful, making someone's day a little lighter — that's where I find real fulfilment. Growth, for me, isn't just personal. It only really means something if it eventually helps someone else too.

I believe in the journey over the destination. I'm ambitious, and I care about the things I set my mind to — but I never want my life to shrink down to a list of achievements. I want to stay curious, kind, open to being wrong, and willing to change my mind. Some of my biggest growth has come from the messy middle of things — the mistakes, the uncertainty, the figuring-it-out — not the neatly wrapped ending.

on my bookshelf

Ask me about Middle-earth or Westeros and I will not be brief about it. J.R.R. Tolkien and George R.R. Martin built worlds so vast and human that I've spent years happily lost in them — not for the dragons or the battles, but for what they say about power, loss, loyalty, and the long, winding journeys their characters take to become who they are. And closer to home, Humayun Ahmed's writing has a different kind of magic — the way he finds depth and quiet emotion in ordinary Bengali life is something I don't think I'll ever stop returning to.
